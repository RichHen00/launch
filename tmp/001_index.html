In mathematics, an operation is simply a rule for taking some numbers and doing something specific with them to get a result. You already know the common ones, like addition and multiplication. Most of these are binary operations, which is just a fancy way of saying they take two numbers as inputs.

While we usually see operations as symbols like plus or minus signs, an operation can actually be anything. Mathematicians often invent their own "custom" operations for a specific problem. They might pick a random symbol, like a hashtag or a star, and define a custom rule for it. For example, if someone tells you that a hashtag means "add the two numbers together and then subtract ten," you just follow that recipe whenever you see the hashtag symbol.

When dealing with these operations, the most important thing is the order of steps. Always start inside the parentheses and work your way out. If there are no parentheses, follow the standard order of operations: handle exponents first, then multiplication and division from left to right, and finally addition and subtraction from left to right. Once you know the rule and the order, you just plug your numbers into the formula and solve.
