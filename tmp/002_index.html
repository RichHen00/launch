In math, an operation is simply a rule for taking some numbers and doing something to them to get a result. You are already familiar with common operations like addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. When an operation takes two numbers as inputs, it is called a binary operation.

While we usually use standard symbols like plus or minus, an operation can be defined in any way the creator wants. Sometimes, mathematicians invent "custom" operations using symbols like a hash or a circle. To solve a problem with a custom operation, you just follow the specific recipe provided. For example, if a rule says that a # b means you add the numbers and then subtract their product, you simply plug your numbers into that formula.

When dealing with complex problems involving multiple operations, follow the standard order of operations. Always solve whatever is inside parentheses first, working from the innermost set outward. If there are no parentheses, handle exponents first, then multiplication and division from left to right, and finally addition and subtraction from left to right.
